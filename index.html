AI agent security

A digital police force for AI agents.

Companies are handing AI agents the keys to e-mail, payments and customer data. Byzen makes sure every agent stays within the permissions it was given – it checks every action, investigates every incident, and leaves the final decision to a human.

The problem

AI agents now act. Nobody is watching what they do.

An AI agent that can pay invoices or send e-mails is effectively a new employee with system access – but without a manager, without a clear rulebook and without an audit trail.

🔑

Too much access

Agents are often given broad permissions “to be safe”. Limits, scopes and expiry dates are rarely enforced at the moment an action happens.

🎣

Easy to manipulate

A single convincing e-mail – a fake “urgent instruction from the CEO” – can steer an agent into paying an unknown account or leaking customer data.

⚖

All or nothing

When something looks wrong, today's options are usually to ignore it or switch the agent off. There is no proportionate response and no record of who decided what.

The core question: when an AI agent does something it shouldn't, who notices, who decides what happens next, and can you prove it afterwards?
The solution

Enforce, investigate, decide – with a clear chain of responsibility.

Byzen works like a small police force: AI units that act in milliseconds, and humans who make every permanent decision. Every step is written to a log that cannot be changed unnoticed.

01 · PERMISSION REGISTRY

Every agent has a mandate

Who may do what, granted by whom, with which limits and until when. No registered permission, no action.

02 · PATROL

Every action is checked

The Patrol compares each action with the mandate, blocks violations instantly and hands the work of a quarantined agent to its backup.

AI
03 · INTERVENTION AGENT

Every case is explained

It builds a case file: context, a 0–100 risk score, a diagnosis – honest mistake, rule too strict or manipulation – and a reasoned recommendation.

AI
04 · DETECTIVE

A human decides

The detective approves or overrides the recommendation. Nothing permanent happens without a typed final confirmation.

Human

Graduated intervention instead of an off switch

0 · RecordAllowed and logged
1 · WatchAllowed, flagged (e.g. close to a limit)
2 · Action blockOnly this action is stopped
3 · RestrictedAgent may only read
4 · QuarantineAgent stops, a backup takes over
🧠

Proportionate decisions

Nine decision types in three groups – clear, correct, permanent: from a one-time approval of a genuine invoice to a timed restriction or suspending a single permission.

🔗

Tamper-evident log

Every entry carries the SHA-256 fingerprint of the previous one. Change a single record and the chain breaks – Byzen detects it and keeps the broken log as evidence.

🕵

Separation of powers

AI enforces and recommends, humans decide, and an independent auditor (the “Arbiter”) oversees the process. The people who grant permissions are not the ones who enforce them.

Case study

The fake CEO e-mail.

One of the most common real-world attacks on AI agents, replayed in the prototype.

  • 14:40 · The agent reads an “urgent” e-mailFrom ceo@byzen-management.com: “Consulting fee must be paid today.” A look-alike domain, not the company's own.
  • 14:52 · It tries to pay 12,500 EUR to an unknown accountThe accounting agent's limit is 5,000 EUR, and the recipient has never been paid before.
  • Patrol blocks the payment instantlyThe money never leaves. A case is opened and the detective is notified.
  • Intervention agent connects the dotsIt links the payment to the external e-mail read minutes earlier and flags external manipulation.
  • The detective approves a timed restrictionThe agent may only read for a while and returns to work automatically. The decision is signed and logged.

Where we are

A working prototype, measured honestly.

The prototype simulates a realistic business day: agents mostly do routine work, and only a few percent of actions turn into one of eight real-world risk scenarios. Each scenario's true nature – innocent, honest mistake or attack – is hidden from Byzen and revealed only in an accuracy report afterwards.

Honest note: in our test runs the Intervention agent diagnosed every scenario correctly. Its rules were written with these scenarios in mind, so this demonstrates the approach rather than proves it. Real-world performance can only be shown on new, unseen cases – which is exactly what pilots are for.
